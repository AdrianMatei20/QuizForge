Probability measures how likely an event is to happen. It is a number from 0 to 1, where 0 means the event is impossible and 1 means it is certain. Probabilities are often written as fractions, decimals or percentages, so a probability of one half can also be written as 0.5 or 50 percent.

When all the outcomes of an experiment are equally likely, the probability of an event is the number of outcomes in which it happens divided by the total number of possible outcomes. A fair six-sided die has six equally likely outcomes, so the probability of rolling a 4 is 1 divided by 6. The probability of rolling an even number is 3 divided by 6, which is one half, because three of the six outcomes are even.

The complement of an event is the event not happening. The probability of an event and the probability of its complement always add up to 1. If the probability of rain tomorrow is 0.3, the probability of no rain is 0.7.

Two events are independent when one happening does not change the probability of the other. For independent events, the probability that both happen is the product of their probabilities. The probability of getting heads twice in two tosses of a fair coin is one half times one half, which is one quarter.

A common mistake, called the gambler's fallacy, is to believe that past results change the probability of independent events. After five heads in a row, the probability of heads on the next toss of a fair coin is still one half.
