---
title: Causes of the French Revolution
count: 5
difficulty: medium

The French Revolution began in 1789. It had several causes that built up over many years.

French society was divided into three estates. The First Estate was the clergy and the Second Estate was the nobility. The Third Estate was everyone else, from rich merchants to poor peasants, and it made up about 97 percent of the population. The first two estates paid almost no taxes, so the tax burden fell on the Third Estate.

By the 1780s the French government was deep in debt. It had spent heavily on wars, including its support for the American War of Independence, and the court of King Louis XVI was expensive to run. Attempts to make the nobility pay more taxes failed.

At the same time, bad harvests in 1788 made bread, the main food of the poor, very expensive. Many families spent most of their income on bread, and hunger made people angry with the government.

New ideas also played a part. Writers of the Enlightenment, such as Rousseau and Montesquieu, argued that power should come from the people and that government should be limited by laws. These ideas made many people question the king's absolute power and the privileges of the nobility.

To solve the money crisis, Louis XVI called a meeting of the Estates-General in May 1789, for the first time since 1614. Each estate had one vote, so the first two estates could always outvote the Third Estate. The deputies of the Third Estate demanded voting by head instead. When this was refused, they declared themselves the National Assembly and swore not to separate until France had a constitution. In July 1789, crowds in Paris stormed the Bastille, a royal prison that had become a symbol of the king's power.
